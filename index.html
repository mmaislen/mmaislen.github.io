Enterprise deals unblocked

Your product is ready for enterprise customers. Their IT team isn’t.

The buyer loves it, but IT keeps sending security questionnaires, and big deals stall for months.

How I’d fix it

I’ll get you to yes: find out exactly what their IT needs, turn it into a plan your team can ship, and see the reviews through to sign-off.

How I did it at Microsoft

Situation
Accenture and Microsoft were ready to roll out Mesh on Meta Quest headsets. IT blocked it: the headsets had never been qualified against enterprise security requirements, and failed sign-ins were customers’ top complaint.
Constraint
The fix spanned Meta and six Microsoft groups: identity, device management, security, IT, Windows and Office. Meta was still building its admin center, the sign-in system wasn’t built for 3D, and a company-wide security push was competing for the same people.
What I did
I treated enterprise readiness as part of the product. I pinned down exactly what IT needed to say yes, found a sign-in fix through another team, ran the security and privacy reviews between Meta and Microsoft (a first for Meta), and carried every approval through to executive sign-off.
Result
Mesh became the first Microsoft 365 app with conditional access on Quest, which ended customers’ top sign-in complaint. Accenture’s rollout was unblocked, and Mesh became the first Microsoft VR immersive events app on Quest. The same playbook became the strategy for Apple Vision Pro.

Your launch back on track

Your launch keeps slipping, and nobody can tell you why.

Every team says it’s waiting on another team, and nothing ships.

How I’d fix it

I’ll get it moving: one owner for the whole experience, one plan and clear handoffs, usually within weeks.

How I did it at Microsoft

Situation
An AI feature in a product used by millions had been stuck for nearly two years. Four engineering teams in four time zones each said they were waiting on someone else.
Constraint
No one owned how the pieces fit together, a fixed deadline was coming, and I had no authority over any of the four teams.
What I did
I treated it as an ownership problem, not an engineering one. I defined the full experience end to end, including what happens when the AI gets it wrong, gave every team one plan and clear handoffs, and got a missing requirement committed on very short notice.
Result
Within two weeks, the teams were working from one plan. The first release shipped two months early, and the feature reached general release on deadline.

You get

Big changes, no customer backlash

Your customers rely on something you need to change.

A pricing change, a migration, a new AI feature that uses their data. Get it wrong and customers leave.

How I’d fix it

I’ll land it safely: the right people signed off up front, every team on one plan, tested with real customers first, and I stay through the pushback.

How I did it at Microsoft

Situation
Teams had to get people’s explicit consent before collecting their location, on desktop, Mac, iOS and Android. Location is sensitive personal data under privacy laws, so getting consent wrong risks regulatory fines and customer trust, and every week of delay adds to that risk. More than ten teams relied on that data, including emergency calling.
Constraint
A careless fix would break 911 calls. Legal and privacy had to approve every word, and customers’ IT teams would feel the change first.
What I did
I got legal and privacy aligned up front, put every dependent team on one plan, and tested the wording with our customer advisory board before launch. When large customers pushed back afterward, I led those conversations, found a fix through device settings they already managed, and traced part of the problem to a Windows bug.
Result
Shipped on time worldwide, including government clouds. The privacy risk was closed before any customer raised it, and the Windows bug was fixed for everyone.
