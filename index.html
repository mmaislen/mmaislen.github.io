A stuck launch

Two months early

A launch stuck for two years, moving in two weeks

How I solved it
Situation
An AI feature in a product used by millions had been stuck for nearly two years. Four engineering teams in four time zones each said they were waiting on someone else.
Constraint
No one owned how the pieces fit together, a fixed deadline was coming, and I had no authority over any of the four teams.
What I did
I treated it as an ownership problem, not an engineering one. I defined the full experience end to end, including what happens when the AI gets it wrong. Then I gave each team one plan and clear handoffs, and got a missing requirement committed on very short notice.
Result
In two weeks the teams were working from one plan. We hit the first release two months early, met the deadline, and shipped in stages to general release.

Enterprise security review

Rollout unblocked

IT said no. Two companies had to say yes.

How I solved it
Situation
Mesh, our immersive events app, couldn't run on Quest headsets without Microsoft 365 sign-in and security in place. Accenture had bought headsets specifically to run it, and its rollout was blocked.
Constraint
Nobody owned making it work. It needed two companies with different goals, and Meta was still building the enterprise admin tools we depended on. I didn't control either company's roadmap.
What I did
I stepped in as the owner. I laid out the goals, blockers, people and timeline on one page, got leadership at both companies behind it, and set the device management strategy. Then I brought Meta's new admin center into Microsoft and took both companies' security and privacy reviews to a yes.
Result
Mesh shipped with conditional access on Quest and a clear way for IT to manage the headsets. That unblocked Accenture's rollout and made Mesh the first Microsoft VR immersive events app available to companies on Quest.

A sensitive change

On time, worldwide

A privacy change across ten teams, without breaking 911

How I solved it
Situation
Teams had to start asking people for consent before using their location, on desktop, Mac, iOS and Android. More than ten teams relied on that data, including emergency calling.
Constraint
A careless fix would break emergency calls. Legal and privacy had to approve every word, and customers' IT teams would feel the change first.
What I did
I owned it end to end. I got legal and privacy to sign off, lined up more than ten engineering and feature teams, tested the wording with our customer advisory board, and wrote the admin and user guidance. When large enterprise customers pushed back after launch, I led those conversations, found a fix through managed-device settings, and traced part of the problem to a Windows bug.
Result
Shipped on time, worldwide, including government clouds. The privacy risk was closed before any customer raised it, and the Windows bug was fixed for every customer.

AI adoption

~200 people trained

From nervous to building their own AI workflows

How I solved it
Situation
I was training colleagues to use an AI agent pilot.
Constraint
I expected technical questions. What I got was nerves. People didn't feel confident enough to try.
What I did
I skipped the architecture and showed one plain-English example: “Schedule a meeting with my manager at 10am tomorrow.”
Result
About 200 people later, teams were building their own workflows.
